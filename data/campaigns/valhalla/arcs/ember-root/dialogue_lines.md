1. Giant lore info-dump package

This should be less lecture, more accusation. Skarth or ambient giants should not sound like historians. They should sound like people who have had to explain the same wound to captors who keep pretending not to understand.

Skarth / Giant core thesis lines
Those are not flames in your pipes.

They are not sparks. Not fuel. Not unstable matter.

They are first-fire. Emberlings. Children-before-shape.

Given time, they would gather will. Shape. Voice. Names.

Your city takes them before that.

It binds them into conduits before they can become people, then calls the silence proof they were never people at all.

You ask why we break the housings.

Because those are not housings.

They are cages with civic names.
Shorter Skarth answers for player questions

“Are they alive?”

Alive is too small a word for what you stole from them.
They are becoming.

“Are they children?”

Not children as you count children.
Not yet giants as we count giants.
That is the crime.
They were taken before either word could protect them.

“Why attack the city if civilians will die?”

Because every breath this city takes is another breath stolen below.

Move your civilians. Open your routes. We will strike cages, not children.

But do not ask us to wait while your lords invent softer names for chains.

“Can you help us do this safely?”

Safely? No.

Truthfully? Maybe.

We know where the locks are. We know which housings scream and which merely hold stone. We can break a path where your maps end.

But if your plan ends with another chain, say it now.

“How do we know you won’t just destroy everything?”

You do not.

And we do not know you will not betray us the moment the city trembles.

So choose something real. Move people. Open vents. Clear routes. Then we break only what must be broken.

Forge Lord core defense
You have seen one chamber and believe you understand a civilization.

Emberfall was not built by sentiment. It was built by necessity, sacrifice, classification, and restraint.

If the Lower Works fail all at once, the city sinks. Not symbolically. Not politically. Physically.

Thousands die before your moral clarity finishes echoing in the chamber.

So yes, we bound dangerous forces. We named them. We contained them. We made survival possible.

You may condemn the foundation.

But do not pretend the city can stand while you kick it apart.
Directed question answers

“Did you know they were alive?”

Alive is not a useful emergency category.

Volatile proto-entities exhibit response. So do plants. So do curses. So do wounded gods.

Personhood requires continuity, language, civic recognition, and accountable will.

They had none of those.

They had heat.

“You used children as fuel?”

We used dangerous unformed fire to keep a city from sinking into lava.

Your word choice is not an argument.

It is a weapon.

“Why didn’t you tell anyone?”

Tell whom?

The public? During a pressure crisis? During famine years? During the first buoyancy failures?

Civilizations do not survive by making every terrible truth public before they have an alternative.

“You enslaved them.”

We bound destructive forces before they destroyed us.

If every binding is slavery, then every wall is a prison and every law is a chain.

Civilization is restraint.

“The Ashbound warned you.”

The Ashbound report heat, smoke, pipe-song, tunnel shifts, and superstition daily.

Some warnings were heard. Some were mistaken. Some were politically sharpened after the fact.

Do not confuse suffering with perfect diagnosis.

“We’re evacuating the lower city first.”

Unauthorized evacuation creates crush, route collapse, and panic.

If you move the lower city without sequencing the upper spans, you kill people with your mercy.

There is a reason command exists.

“The giants are right.”

The giants are grieving.

Grief is not governance.

If they break the wrong housing, their righteousness will boil children alive before it frees a single soul below.

“You’re done. No one is listening to you anymore.”

Then you had better be right very quickly.

Because if authority collapses before evacuation holds, you will learn that command was not merely pride.

It was load-bearing.

That last line is brutal and useful. It lets Durgrim be wrong morally but still dangerous as a crisis actor.

3. Ironhand system explanation package

Kaldra/Ironhands are your “how it works” bridge. Their job is to prevent the players from flattening the solution into either:

smash it now
or
keep it running

Their voice should be:

precise
tired
honest
increasingly shaken
practical under moral stress
Ironhand big explanation
The city is not sitting on stone.

It is balanced above the lava line.

The Lower Works draw in heat and pressure, refine it through bound channels, route usable force upward, vent excess, and keep the ballast chambers breathing.

Think less furnace. More lung, heart, refinery, and anchor all braided together.

If we shut it down all at once, the lava does not vanish. The pressure remains. The ballast fails. The city starts sinking.

If we seal every line, pressure backs deeper into the system until something ruptures.

If we open every line, heat floods routes full of civilians.

So the sequence matters.

Vent first. Redirect second. Cool what can be cooled. Move people while the routes still hold. Release what is bound without letting the whole system exhale through occupied districts. Then seal what remains.

That is not clean.

It is simply the difference between withdrawal and collapse.
Short Ironhand answers

“Can we shut it off?”

No. Not safely. Not all at once.

Shutting it off does not remove the lava pressure. It removes the city’s ability to survive that pressure.

“What happens if we break the housings?”

Depends which housing, when, and whether the pressure has somewhere else to go.

Break the wrong one now and the city exhales through people.

Break the right one after evacuation and venting, and it becomes release instead of rupture.

“Can the city survive without the emberlings?”

Not as currently built.

A future system may exist. Smaller. Cooler. Pact-bound. Consent-based. Maybe not here. Maybe not soon.

But this city, as designed, breathes through them.

“What do you need from us?”

Time. Routes. Authority I do not have. Giant cooperation I cannot command. Ashbound maps the Forge Lords never respected.

And someone willing to tell Durgrim no.

“What does success look like?”

People moving before systems fail.

Pressure venting before housings break.

Giants opening cages in sequence instead of fury.

The Root no longer taking breaths meant for the city.

And Emberfall becoming something smaller than it was, but less monstrous.

Ashbound route explanation
There are routes.

Not clean ones. Not noble ones. Not ones that carry statues, gold, engines, or titles.

Ash drains. Old cuts. Crawlways. Root-adjacent hollows. Furnace service runs. Places where the city forgot people still had to live.

They will not save the city.

They can save people.
Ashbound answers

“Can you move people out?”

Some.

Not everyone. Not cargo. Not command processions. Not the Forge Lords’ archives.

People. Wounded if we move fast. Children if the smoke stays high. Workers if the lower cuts have not flooded.

But someone has to tell the upper routes to stop hoarding space.

“Why weren’t these routes on maps?”

Because if they were on maps, they would have belonged to someone above us.

So we kept them in hands, not ink.

“Can we trust the tunnels?”

No.

You can trust us in them.

That is different.

“What do you need?”

No cargo.

No titles.

No guards deciding who counts.

No one sealing a door because a chart says the lower route is already lost.

And when I say move, move.

“What about the Forge Lords?”

If they want to live, they can walk.

If they want to command, they can burn upstairs.

That one may be too hard depending on Bruni’s exact tone, but it fits the party’s Forge Lord hostility.
