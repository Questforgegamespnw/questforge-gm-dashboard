# Session 1 — The City Burns

> Emberfall Entry

## Purpose
- Introduce multi-objective pressure.
- Establish failure through prioritization.
- Reinforce that players cannot solve everything.
- Begin shifting reality from ordered to unstable.

## Design Truth
Players should leave thinking: “That was a lot.”

But also: “I chose what I needed to lose.”

## Reality Layers

### Player Reality
- “We’re here to save the city.”
- “We just need to stabilize things.”
- “If we act fast enough, we can win this.”

### Observed Weirdness
- Fires resist normal suppression.
- Sparks fall too slowly.
- Heat changes randomly.
- Voices echo strangely.

### DM Truth
- The system is already collapsing.
- The Root is amplifying failure.
- Players are assigning losses, not preventing loss.

## Opening — Bifrost Drop Into Emberfall
The drop is too steep, too hot, too late, and not clean or controlled.

## Encounter Structure

### Phase 0 — Arrival Shock
Disorientation, immediate stakes, no clean battlefield. Do not ask “What do you do?” Ask “Who are you going for?”

### Phase 1 — Control Attempt
Let players act effectively. Present 2–3 minor problems, all individually solvable.

### Phase 2 — System Failure
After 2–3 rounds or one stabilized objective, trigger a new failure: chainway collapse, fire surge, or civilian choke.

### Phase 3 — Multi-Front Crisis
Force prioritization through a new hazard, objective, and NPC demand.

| Pressure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Civilians | Moral pressure |
| Structure | Future consequence |
| Fire spread | Time pressure |

### Phase 4 — Partial Stabilization
Some are saved, some are dead, some things are worse.

## Final Emotional Beat
A survivor says: “You came… just in time.” Beat. “…for some of us.”
