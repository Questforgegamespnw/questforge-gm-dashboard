# Player-Specific Hallucinations

> “This is not happening to the party. It is happening to you.”

## How to Use These

- Trigger 1 per session, maximum 2
- Target one player at a time
- Keep it ambiguous
- Do not confirm whether others see it
- Never fully resolve it immediately

## Delivery Rule

Say:

> “Only you notice this.”

Then play it out.

## Scene 1 — The Wrong Memory

Universal.

### Trigger

- Quiet moment
- Investigation pause
- Walking through town

### Scene

Tell a player:

> “You remember this street differently.”

Then describe:

- A building that is not there anymore
- A conversation they remember having
- A path that feels familiar

### Twist

If they follow it:

- It leads somewhere wrong
- Or nowhere

### Purpose

Plants doubt in their own memory.

## Scene 2 — The Other You

Useful for rogue, bard, artificer, or anyone with strong self-concept.

### Trigger

- Reflection
- Still water
- Mirror

### Scene

> “Your reflection does not match you.”

Then:

- It moves differently
- Holds a different posture
- Maybe looks more confident

Optional whispered line:

> “You could have been better.”

### Twist

If interacted with, it mirrors them, then stops when they do not.

### Purpose

Introduces alternate-self concept and Echo Boss tie-in.

## Scene 3 — The Bard / Loki Thread

### Trigger

- After Loki appearance
- During quiet moment

### Scene

The Bard hears multiple voices speaking at once.

Then they speak, and it comes out as someone else’s voice.

Line in another voice:

> “Which one is yours?”

### Twist

For one moment, they cannot control which voice comes out.

### Purpose

Identity destabilization and Loki influence escalation.

## Scene 4 — The Failed Moment

Good for Paladin or heroic characters.

### Trigger

- After combat
- During rest

### Scene

They see:

- A moment where they failed
- Someone dying they could not save

Voice:

> “You chose wrong.”

### Twist

It did not happen, but feels like it did.

### Purpose

Reinforces Ashroot consequences and emotional weight.

## Scene 5 — The Artificer / Corewright Tie-In

### Trigger

- Near constructs
- Near Root energy

### Scene

They see a golem they created acting independently.

It says:

> “You built me to serve.”
>
> “Why do you get to choose what I am?”

### Twist

The golem is not real, but uses their design logic.

### Purpose

Ties into:

- Living Core
- System vs autonomy
- Endgame themes

## Scene 6 — The Rogue / Swashbuckler

### Trigger

- Alone moment
- Scouting
- Sneaking

### Scene

They see someone moving exactly like them.

Same stance. Same rhythm.

Line:

> “You’re predictable.”

### Twist

The double anticipates their movement and blocks their path.

### Purpose

Undermines confidence and sets up Echo combat.

## Scene 7 — The Group

High impact.

### Trigger

- All players together
- Quiet moment

### Scene

> “For a split second… you are not all here.”

Then one player is:

- Missing
- Replaced
- Slightly different

Then it snaps back.

### Purpose

Group-level paranoia.

Theme:

> “We might not all be the same people.”

## Paladin Hallucination — The Empty Victory

> “You won. So why does this feel like defeat?”

### Trigger Options

Use when:

- After a successful combat
- During a quiet moment after Ashroot consequences
- Right before or after the Echo Self fight
- When the Paladin confidently asserts control

### Setup

Tell them:

> “Only you notice this.”

### Vision

The battlefield is silent and still.

Victory is obvious.

Enemies lie defeated.

The party stands victorious.

But no one celebrates.

### Details That Feel Wrong

- The air is too still
- No wind, fire, or movement
- Allies stand but do not react

Everyone is waiting for the Paladin.

### Shift

One ally turns to them with a neutral expression.

Line:

> “You won.”
>
> “Isn’t this what you wanted?”

### Escalation

More bodies appear than expected.

Not just enemies.

Among them:

- Civilians
- Allies
- Possibly someone recognized

Layered voice:

> “Victory requires sacrifice.”

### Personal Strike

The Paladin looks down at their weapon.

It is radiant, but dripping with something darker than blood.

Final line:

> “Would you still choose it… if this is what it costs?”

### Snap Back

- Sound returns instantly
- Party continues as normal
- No one else noticed

### Optional Mechanical Effect

Weight of Victory.

For the next turn or next combat round, let the player choose one:

- Disadvantage on first attack
- Advantage, but deals extra damage to anyone adjacent, friend or foe

### Purpose

This is not about fear.

It is about questioning what victory means.

It challenges:

- Conquest philosophy
- Ends justify the means
- Certainty in dominance

## Follow-Up Callback

Later, an NPC or Loki may say:

> “You’ve already seen what winning looks like.”

## Final Note

This hallucination should leave the player feeling:

- Powerful
- Unsettled
- Thoughtful

Not punished or weak.
