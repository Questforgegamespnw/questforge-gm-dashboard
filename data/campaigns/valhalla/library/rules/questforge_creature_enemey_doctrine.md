# Questforge Creature / Enemy Doctrine

If a force is sentient, it has motives.
It has reasons.
It has pressure behind its actions.
It has a deeper truth that can be discovered, negotiated with, exploited, challenged, or understood.

If a force is not sentient, it is reacting.
It responds to hunger, fear, pain, territory, instinct, corruption, imbalance, or environmental pressure.

If a non-sentient force appears organized, targeted, or symbolic, then something sentient may be directing, shaping, exploiting, or provoking it.

Therefore:
Enemies do not exist merely to be fought.
They exist because something in the world is wounded, hungry, ambitious, afraid, controlled, trapped, or wrong.

That doctrine is exactly what happened with the giants.

At first glance:

Huge fire giant attacking city infrastructure.

Deeper truth:

A sentient exploited people breaking open the machinery that enslaved their young.

That also gives you a really useful encounter design checklist:

Is this enemy sentient?
- What do they want?
- What are they afraid of?
- What do they believe justifies their actions?
- What would make them stop, talk, redirect, retreat, or escalate?

Is this enemy non-sentient?
- What stimulus is it reacting to?
- What natural behavior is being misread as malice?
- What pain, hunger, territory, corruption, or imbalance drives it?

Is someone controlling it?
- Who benefits?
- What signal, ritual, command, bargain, infection, or system is shaping its behavior?

That’s why your players are learning not to kick every door open. The world has trained them that asking “why?” is often more powerful than swinging first.

## Questforge Encounter Doctrine — Inquiry Before Violence

That is a huge strength, but it does create a prep obligation:

If enemies have motives, players will ask about motives.
If systems create monsters, players will ask about systems.
If violence has context, players will look for the context before choosing violence.

That means your future prep needs less “stat block first” and more answer bank first.

The table is being intentionally trained to ask why.

When a threat appears, players should be rewarded for asking:
- What is it doing?
- Why is it doing that?
- Who benefits from this?
- What pain, fear, command, hunger, pressure, or belief is driving it?
- What would make this stop without violence?
- What would make violence necessary anyway?

Combat should not be the default assumption.
Combat should be the chosen answer after the party understands enough to decide that force is acceptable, necessary, useful, or aligned with their goals.

When combat breaks out, it should carry weight because the players are not merely clearing enemies.
They are accepting a cost.

## For your dashboard/content model, this points directly at the importance of answerMoments.

Not every actor needs a novel. But every major sentient force should have fast answers for:

What do they want?
What are they afraid of?
What are they doing right now?
What do they think justifies it?
What would make them stop?
What would make them escalate?
What truth are they carrying that the party may not know?

And every non-sentient or environmental threat should answer:

What is it reacting to?
What stimulus changes its behavior?
What is being mistaken for malice?
What sentient force, if any, is exploiting or directing it?

That is the real evolution here. Your prep is shifting from “encounter content” to interrogable world content.
