# Fear System / Fear vs Corruption Loop

## Purpose

Make fear tactical rather than disabling.

Fear should:

- Create movement
- Shape the battlefield
- Avoid stagnation
- Avoid panic-lock
- Stay unified across spell types and fear-like effects

## Core Fear Rule

Enemies receive fear saves at key pressure points:

1. Initial application
2. At half HP
3. When line of sight breaks

This creates persistent psychological pressure without freezing the fight permanently.

## Frightened Behavior

Frightened creatures do not simply stop playing.

They:

- Reposition
- Retreat intelligently
- Avoid direct threat lines
- Seek cover
- Break line of sight if they can
- Move in ways that create tactical change

## DM Lever

Use fear to:

- Create movement
- Shape the battlefield
- Split enemies from strong positions
- Keep combat from becoming static

## Corruption Breach Rule

When a Corruption Node pulses or a Rift activates, all affected enemies immediately reroll fear saves with advantage.

## Why This Works

This does not invalidate fear-based characters.

It creates an ebb and flow:

1. Control establishes
2. Corruption disrupts
3. Chaos returns
4. Control can be reasserted

## Example Loop

### Moment 1 — Control

The Paladin advances. Aura pressure hits. Enemies fail fear saves.

Narration:

> “They won’t look at you.”
>
> “They back away, shielding their eyes.”
>
> “Something in them knows they should not be here.”

### Moment 2 — Disruption

A Corruption Node pulses. Ice cracks. Whispering voices rise.

Affected enemies reroll fear saves with advantage.

One or two break free.

### Moment 3 — Chaos Returns

The brute charges again. The Skald repositions. The Bard reacts. The Rogue adapts.

### Moment 4 — Reassertion

The Paladin re-engages. Fear reapplies. Control returns.

## Design Truth

Mechanics reinforce fantasy.

Do not hide the fear effect. Spotlight it in narration.
