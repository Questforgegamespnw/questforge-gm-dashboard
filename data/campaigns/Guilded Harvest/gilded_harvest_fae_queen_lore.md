# The Gilded Harvest — Fae Queen Lore Dossier

## Working Names

Use whichever name best fits the final tone of the module.

- **Mistress Harrowell** — mortal-facing identity; elegant spouse of the long-winning farmer.
- **The Mercantile Matron** — GM-facing role title.
- **The Candle Queen** — rumor/title among fae rivals.
- **The Queen of the Long Road** — older, more mythic title tied to the retired adventurer’s prototype bargain.
- **The Gilded Matron** — polished adventure-facing title.

Default cockpit name: **Mistress Harrowell, the Mercantile Matron**.

---

## Core Identity

Mistress Harrowell is a fae queen/matron who has spent years embedded in the mortal town surrounding the Gilded Harvest festival. Publicly, she is known as the refined, successful, slightly too-polished spouse of the Harrowell farmer, whose family has won the grand harvest prizes for five consecutive years.

Privately, she is a fae sovereign who has built a new method of power: not through old grand bargains, stolen names, changelings, impossible curses, or firstborn trades, but through thousands of tiny, pleasant, almost harmless transactions.

She sells candles.

Each sale is a tiny bargain. Each candle is a receipt. Each buyer gives coin, attention, courtesy, and a shaving of memory. In return, they receive warmth, comfort, beauty, and access to a cherished sensory memory while the candle lasts.

Individually, the exchange is barely noticeable.

At festival scale, it is immense.

---

## Public Persona

Mistress Harrowell is perceived as a “made it big” farmer’s wife: elegant, wealthy, gracious, and a bit above the town’s usual mud-and-straw sensibilities. She is not openly cruel or cold. If anything, she is too pleasant.

She is known for:

- Selling bespoke scented candles during the Gilded Harvest.
- Dressing with courtly refinement despite being attached to a farm family.
- Knowing names, preferences, and emotional weak points with unnerving precision.
- Speaking gently and never seeming hurried.
- Remaining composed during conflicts.
- Answering questions very carefully.
- Looking almost unchanged despite the passing years.

Local gossip treats her agelessness like a compliment.

> “Mistress Harrowell hasn’t aged a day in five years. Which is a compliment, obviously. Mostly.”

That line should land as harmless small-town gossip early, then become damning later.

## The Candle Trade

### Surface Read

Her candles are one of the Gilded Harvest’s beloved annual attractions. They are beautiful, personal, expensive, and sell incredibly fast. People line up for them. Many visitors return each year specifically to buy another.

Most candles are genuinely lovely.

They may smell like:

- A first love’s lipstick.
- A newborn baby’s skin.
- Fresh rain on a childhood roof.
- Grandmother’s kitchen.
- A father’s pipe smoke.
- Summer hay.
- A wedding morning.
- The sea seen for the first time.
- Clean linen after illness.
- A favorite horse’s mane.
- Festival apples and warm bread.
- Home before home changed.

The memory remains available while the candle lasts.

This is why people love them.

The product is genuinely good.

### The Hidden Weirdness

The more the party looks, the less the candle business makes sense.

- She sells an impossible volume of candles.
- Purchasers do not always remember the conversation clearly.
- People remember wanting one, buying one, and feeling pleased afterward, but not the middle of the exchange.
- The Harrowell farm is too small to support the production scale.
- No one can identify the workshop.
- There is no visible wax storage, scenting room, packaging operation, or delivery infrastructure that explains the volume.
- Her candles are too personally accurate.
- The stock seems to last longer than it should.
- The candles may briefly burn green-gold when first lit.

### What the Candles Actually Are

The candles are both:

1. **Glamour anchors** — they help maintain and reinforce her mortal identity as Mistress Harrowell.
2. **Bargain receipts** — each sale records a tiny fae exchange.

Each candle says, in fae-law terms:

> I met her.  
> I accepted from her hand.  
> I gave something.  
> I received something.  
> I did not object.  
> The exchange was settled.

The word **settled** matters.

It means paid, closed, recorded, and uncontested.

---

## The Trigger Phrase

Mistress Harrowell’s soft glamour is built around courtesy, not command.

Her common sales line is:

> “Won’t you please buy a candle?”

It is not a crude domination spell. It is not obvious charm magic. It works because refusing would be socially awkward, especially when she has already read the buyer’s fatigue, want, grief, or longing.

Her strongest spells sound like manners.

### Fae Glamour Doctrine

Fae glamour does not automatically provoke a roll when it is subtle, polite, and socially plausible. A character may challenge it once they have contradiction, suspicion, discomfort, or evidence that the interaction does not add up.

At the stall, do **not** ask for a save unless a player actively resists, challenges, or investigates.

The magic works like a social illusion: it holds until someone has reason to question it.

## Signature Fireable Moment: The Long Road Candles

### Setup

The adventurers have recently returned from a rough weeklong drake hunt. They are exhausted, road-worn, and expecting the Gilded Harvest security job to be paid recovery.

During festival setup or early exploration, Mistress Harrowell briefly addresses them.

> “Long road?” she asks. “Longer than you expected, I think.”

Then:

> “Won’t you please buy a candle?”

Do not linger. Do not call for a roll. Let the moment pass like flavor.

### Evening Delivery

That evening at the Copper Kettle, a server delivers cream-wrapped candles to the party’s rooms. Each has a tiny copper tag stamped:

> **LONG ROAD**

The server says:

> “From Mistress Harrowell. She said you’d settled for them.”

If questioned:

> “I don’t know the details, ser. She keeps very tidy accounts.”

The party remembers the question. They do not clearly remember buying the candles.

### Scent Reveal

When unwrapped, the candles smell of:

> Smoke.  
> Ash.  
> Dragonfire.

Not symbolic dragonfire. The exact memory of the road they just survived: oil and sweat worked into armor straps, charred bark, burned grass, hot scale, old blood, scorched leather, and the silence after the drake fell.

The candle has not captured a scent.

It has kept a receipt.

### GM Truth

The Long Road candles are not an accident. Mistress Harrowell deliberately tips her hand to the party. She has identified them as useful outsiders: guild-backed, capable, armed, recently tested by danger, and not already entangled in local farm loyalties or fae court politics.

The scent is her first recruitment move.

She wants the party curious, unsettled, and aware that something larger than farm rivalry is unfolding.

## The New Currency of Courtesy

Mistress Harrowell has pioneered a new form of fae contract craft: thousands of tiny, pleasant, nearly harmless exchanges instead of rare ruinous bargains.

Old fae power is built on dramatic deals:

- True names.
- Firstborn children.
- Stolen years.
- Changelings.
- Impossible promises.
- Poisoned gifts.
- Oaths sworn under moonlight.
- Bloodline curses.

Her method is different:

- A candle.
- A smile.
- A small coin.
- A pleasant memory.
- A polite request.
- A settled account.

She does not rob the bank.

She takes a penny from every transaction and multiplies it by thousands.

To mortals, this looks like commerce.

To fae law, it is a vast seasonal contract engine.

### Her Philosophy

Mistress Harrowell believes her method is kinder, cleaner, and more efficient than the old ways.

Her core argument:

> “We have always been transactional. I have merely made it mercantile at scale.”

When challenged, she may say:

> “This is nicer, is it not? A candle. Harmless. Warm. Paid for. Settled.”

Or:

> “No name taken. No cradle emptied. No child led away under moonlight. I sold candles.”

This is the heart of her moral problem.

She is right.

And still wrong.

## The Harrowell Farm Bargain

The Harrowell family has won the grand harvest prize for roughly five years straight. Their rivals suspect cheating, but inspectors and judges find no illegal magical enhancement.

The Harrowells can truthfully say:

> “No spell touched the crop. No potion fed the soil. No seed was altered.”

That is because the crops themselves are not magically modified.

The field is.

The Harrowell fields are partially operating under Feywild conditions. Growth is accelerated, robust, beautiful, and otherworldly, but still technically “organic” under the contest rules because no direct spell, alchemical treatment, enchanted fertilizer, or modified seed stock is applied to the crops.

It is a fae-law loophole.

### The Bargain Terms

The Harrowells receive:

- Fertile fields.
- Stronger crops.
- Consistent winning entries.
- Economic survival and social prestige.

Mistress Harrowell receives:

- A percentage of the harvest.
- A mortal anchor for her boundary craft.
- Social cover as the farmer’s spouse.
- A yearly festival population for candle commerce.
- A growing web of settled micro-bargains.

The Harrowells are not cartoon villains. They may see themselves as survivors.

A useful line for the guilty farmer:

> “We did not cheat. We survived.”

## The Fae Court Betrayal

Mistress Harrowell’s partner/consort in the Feywild has decided she has “gone native.” Publicly, he frames her mortal life as shameful: she has debased herself by playing farmer’s wife, selling candles, accepting mortal coin, and rooting her authority in a village festival.

Privately, he fears the balance of power has shifted.

Her candle economy is working.

She has built a renewable power base outside the old court hierarchy, rooted in recognition, courtesy, repeated consent, and mass participation.

That threatens him.

It threatens the old fae political order.

### The Early Harvest Theft

The theft of the prize crops before judging is not merely sabotage. It is a courtly test.

Her consort arranges or authorizes the early taking of the Harrowell prize crops before the tithe is due.

To mortals, it looks like theft.

To the fae court, it is testimony.

If Mistress Harrowell cannot stop or answer the early taking, her enemies can claim she no longer controls:

- Her bargain.
- Her boundary.
- Her tithe.
- Her mortal anchor.
- Her own court authority.

The theft becomes legal and political ammunition for deposition.

### Consort’s Public Argument

> “She has gone native.”

> “She has traded crowns for coppers.”

> “A queen does not weigh sovereignty in candle stubs.”

> “You were given a court, and you built a shop.”

> “A throne rooted in mortal gratitude is still rooted in mud.”

### Consort’s Private Fear

> She is becoming too powerful without him.

He wants to depose her, seize or redirect her court, and align power through a new marriage with another fae house.

## The Retired Adventurer Connection

### Role

The retired adventurer is not guilty of the current farm bargain.

They are a survivor of Mistress Harrowell’s earlier work.

Years ago, before she perfected her candle economy, the Matron tested a rougher form of hospitality bargain. It was less polished, less mercantile, and still half-shaped like the old ways.

### The Prototype Bargain: Safe Passage

The retired adventurer and their companions were lost in a Feywild borderland. They were exhausted, injured, and desperate for a road home. The Matron offered safe passage by candlelight.

The terms sounded merciful:

> Carry my light. Follow my road. Leave behind only what you can spare.

The bargain was imprecise.

They left behind names.

Not their own name, necessarily, but the names of companions. The names that anchored the journey. The names that made grief and love know where to land.

One of those names belonged to the adventurer’s lover or spouse.

### The Missing Spouse

The adventurer has the unmistakable impression of a marriage, but no clear memory of the person.

Possible signs:

- A ring groove or tan line on one finger.
- A reflexive habit of touching an empty ring finger.
- Setting two cups instead of one.
- Turning toward someone who is not there.
- A house arranged for two despite being occupied by one.
- A second chair that is never used.
- Clothing in a chest that does not fit them.
- A wider bed than needed.
- A pressed flower in a book with no remembered context.
- A wedding band in a drawer they insist is not theirs.
- A half-remembered promise to come home.

Useful line:

> “I remember promising to come back.”

Then:

> “I do not remember who I promised.”

### Why They Settled Nearby

Their settlement near the Gilded Harvest is not coincidence.

Mistress Harrowell quietly guided or allowed their life to bend toward the region so she could keep watch over her early work.

They are:

- A survivor.
- A loose thread.
- A living witness.
- A damaged prototype.
- A possible liability.
- A long-running observation subject.

Her motives are mixed:

- Guilt.
- Control.
- Curiosity.
- Research.
- Damage containment.

From her perspective, she has been monitoring a flaw.

From a mortal perspective, that is chilling.

## Retired Adventurer Fireable Moments

### The Empty Ring Finger

**Trigger:** The adventurer smells Matron wax, hears “Long Road,” sees green-gold candlelight, or nears the boundary field.

> Their hand moves before their face changes. Thumb to ring finger. A slow rub over skin where no ring sits.  
>  
> “I was married,” they say.  
>  
> Then their eyes sharpen with panic.  
>  
> “Wasn’t I?”

### The Body Remembers the Crown

**Trigger:** The retired adventurer first sees Mistress Harrowell directly, hears her true voice, or witnesses her glamour slip.

> The retired adventurer goes still. Their face shows confusion before fear. Then, before they seem to understand what they are doing, one knee lowers toward the floor.  
>  
> Their hand catches the table edge halfway down.  
>  
> “No,” they whisper. “No, why would I—”  
>  
> Their eyes lift to her.  
>  
> “Why do I know to kneel?”

Lighter versions:

- They bow without meaning to.
- They lower their eyes when she speaks.
- They step aside before realizing she was behind them.
- They call her “my lady” and immediately look embarrassed.
- They cannot comfortably turn their back on her.

### Don’t Answer Yet

**Trigger:** Mistress Harrowell asks a polite question too gently.

> The retired adventurer grips the nearest sleeve.  
>  
> “Don’t answer yet.”

### The Wax Recognition

**Trigger:** The retired adventurer smells one of her candles.

> “No. No, not that wax. I know that wax.”

### She Is Better At It Now

**Trigger:** The party connects the old safe-passage bargain to the current candle trade.

> “She’s better at it now.”

If asked what they mean:

> “Taking little things. Making it feel like nothing. Back then it hurt more.”

## Mask-Off Confrontation: “Enjoyed the Candle?”

### Trigger

Use when the party confronts Mistress Harrowell about the Long Road candles, missing purchase memory, impossible scent, or candle glamour.

### Core Beat

She should not deny it.

A lesser villain denies.

A fae queen reframes.

### Read-Aloud / Dialogue

> Mistress Harrowell listens without interrupting. The whole time, her expression remains pleasant, almost fond. Only when you finish does she tilt her head and ask:  
>  
> “Enjoyed the candle?”  
>  
> For half a heartbeat, something slips. Not the room. Not exactly. Her. The smile remains, but it belongs to something older than the woman wearing it. Her shadow lengthens across the floor in the shape of branches and a crown. The scent of warm beeswax becomes green rain, autumn leaves, and distant thunder.  
>  
> Then she is only Mistress Harrowell again.  
>  
> “Things are more complicated than you realize.”

### Key Lines

> “Charming? Such an ugly word for being asked nicely.”

> “No name taken. No cradle emptied. No child led away under moonlight.”

> “This is nicer, is it not?”

> “We have always been transactional. I have merely made it mercantile at scale.”

> “Because you finish difficult roads.”

> “Soon, something will be taken before it is owed. When that happens, understand: it is not theft. It is testimony.”

### Recruitment Angle

The Long Road candles are her first recruitment move.

She has clocked the consort’s machinations and is consolidating a response team.

She wants outsiders who are:

- Skilled.
- Armed.
- Guild-backed.
- Recently tested.
- Not local partisans.
- Not already compromised by fae court loyalties.

If the party asks why she tipped her hand:

> “Because you finish difficult roads.”

If she is more direct:

> “My court is moving against me. If they succeed, your pumpkin contest will be the least of what crosses over.”

## Personality and Portrayal

### Voice

Warm, precise, courtly, and lightly amused. She rarely raises her voice. She speaks as though every word has been checked against a contract before it leaves her mouth.

### Behavior Patterns

- Finishes players’ thoughts.
- Uses politeness as leverage.
- Never lies directly if she can mislead by omission.
- Appears exactly when being discussed.
- Treats commerce like ritual.
- Treats ritual like accounting.
- Treats accounting like sovereignty.
- Is more comfortable admitting morally dubious truths than being called sloppy.

### Tell Signs

- Her shadow sometimes resembles branches and a crown.
- Candleflames bend toward her.
- Animals lower their heads without understanding why.
- People step aside before seeing her.
- The retired adventurer bows or nearly kneels.
- Her reflection may show a courtly figure rather than the farmer’s wife.
- Green-gold candlelight reveals too much.

---

## Moral Position

Mistress Harrowell should not be played as purely innocent or purely villainous.

She has:

- Manipulated mortal perception.
- Helped the Harrowells exploit the crop contest.
- Skimmed memory traces from thousands of customers.
- Monitored a damaged prototype victim for years.
- Built power through consent that is technically real but socially engineered.

She has also:

- Avoided the worst old fae predations.
- Given people genuinely meaningful candles.
- Helped a farm family survive.
- Developed a less destructive fae economy.
- Become a target of a more traditional and likely harsher fae power.
- Chosen the party because she needs help stopping something worse.

Her current method is gentler than the old ways.

That does not make it harmless.

Core thread:

> Kindness can still be extraction.

## Scene Hooks and Uses

### Early Festival Hook

The party sees people buying candles in large numbers. Customers leave happy, slightly dazed, and unable to fully describe the conversation.

### Vendor Logistics Hook

A worker comments:

> “She sells enough candles to stock a cathedral vigil, but I’ve never seen a workshop, storehouse, or cart big enough to explain it.”

### Happy Candle Hook

A townsperson says:

> “Mine smelled like my mother’s kitchen. I hadn’t thought of that place in years.”

This makes the candle trade feel beloved before it feels sinister.

### Player Candle Hook

The party receives **Long Road** candles that smell like smoke, ash, dragonfire, armor oil, sweat, charred trees, and the drake road.

## Connected Factions

### The Harrowell Family

Mortal beneficiaries of the field bargain. They have won for five years straight and insist they are not using illegal magic. Technically, they are correct.

### Rival Farm Families

They are angry, suspicious, and economically harmed by the Harrowells’ winning streak. Their accusations are largely correct in spirit and wrong in detail.

### Festival Judges

They enforce written contest rules. Their tests find no direct magical enhancement because the crops themselves are not enchanted.

### The Gilded Harvest Festival

The festival provides the Matron’s perfect seasonal engine: thousands of visitors, emotional commerce, hospitality rituals, and repeated tiny bargains.

### The Fae Consort / King

Her partner and political rival. He claims she has gone native, but secretly fears her new mercantile method has shifted the balance of power.

### The Retired Adventurer

A damaged survivor of her earlier prototype bargain. They are proof that her method was once rougher and more dangerous.

### The Adventuring Party

Potential recruits. The Long Road candles are designed to identify, unsettle, and draw them into the deeper fae-court conflict.

## GM Doctrine

1. Do not reveal her as an accidental villain. She is too competent for that.
2. The Long Road candle reveal is intentional recruitment, not a mistake.
3. Do not call for saves at the first polite candle pitch unless players actively resist or investigate.
4. Let the party discover the missing purchase memory later.
5. Most candles are beautiful and beloved. That makes the extraction more morally complex.
6. The retired adventurer’s body remembers what their mind cannot.
7. The farm bargain obeys written rules while violating fairness.
8. The early crop theft is political testimony, not ordinary theft.
9. The consort’s “gone native” accusation is partly ideology and partly fear.
10. Mistress Harrowell may be manipulative and exploitative, but she may still be the better option.

## One-Paragraph GM Summary

Mistress Harrowell is a fae queen living as the elegant spouse of the festival’s long-winning farmer. Through a hidden bargain, her family’s fields grow under Feywild conditions while still technically obeying the harvest contest’s organic rules. Her true innovation, however, is the candle trade: thousands of tiny, polite, memory-scented purchases that function as glamour anchors and bargain receipts, strengthening her identity and realm through mercantile fae power at scale. Her consort fears this new power base and moves to depose her by stealing the prize crops before the tithe is due, turning a farm scandal into fae-court testimony. The retired adventurer nearby is a survivor of her earlier, rougher safe-passage bargain, having lost the names of companions and perhaps a spouse. The party’s Long Road candles are not a mistake; they are her first recruitment move.
