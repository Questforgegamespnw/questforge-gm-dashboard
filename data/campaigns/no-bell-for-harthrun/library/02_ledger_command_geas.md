# Ledger Command-Geas

## Core Principle

The compromised ledger is not a source of generic corruption and does not transform everyone who sees it. It is a command vector. The demon writes through linked or copied military records in order to make instructions arrive through a format, vocabulary, and chain of authority the recipient already trusts.

## Exposure

Meaningful exposure occurs when someone:

- reads a complete targeted command;
- answers through a linked book;
- copies compromised text into another official record;
- transmits the command through an allied channel;
- accepts the book as authoritative and acts upon it.

A passing glance at meaningless marks is not enough. Use Ledger Influence to represent the network learning names, roles, expected phrasing, and reachable channels.

## Commands at the Table

Commands should be short, plausible, and role-aware: **hold position**, **open the gate**, **stand relieved**, **escort the prisoners**, **await inspection**. The threat is strongest when obedience initially feels like a reasonable military reflex.

Use an appropriate save or curse-resistance ruling for direct supernatural compulsion. Do not remove player agency without warning. Show intrusive authority, allow resistance, and make stronger effects follow deeper or repeated exposure.

## The Subsumed Captain

The captain was a field patrol commander from another village. He is not a silent thrall. The demon preserves his speech, tactical reasoning, and recognizable authority because they make him useful as a local relay. His personal will is submerged beneath the command effect.

If the party uses powerful magic or a spirited solution to break the effect, let it work. Give him one unmistakable human instant. The demon then attempts to impale him and convert him directly, both to reclaim the asset and to begin the direct boss phase. If the party has an extraordinary way to prevent that reaction, allow the captain to survive; the encounter can continue because the network or demon remains threatened.

## Containment

Burning or breaking the chamber ledger stops that physical node, but may not erase commands already copied or transmitted. Safe containment may require:

- isolating all linked copies;
- ending active magical linkage;
- forbidding transcription or reply;
- quarantining exposed communication routes;
- magically cleansing affected officers or records.

The aftermath should establish whether the party destroyed a book, contained a network, or unknowingly carried the breach home.
