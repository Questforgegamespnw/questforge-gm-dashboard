# Ichor Spike Conversion

## The Process

Conversion begins when a black-red demonic spike is driven through a victim's back and holds the body upright. Cursed ichor enters the wound and branches through blood, nerve, and muscle. Over approximately one hour, the victim's living responses are replaced by the Legion's command structure.

The spikes are not laboratory apparatus. Thralls hold a victim in place while the demon's ichor gathers beneath them, rises into a black-red point, and drives through the back. Dormant spikes appear matte and almost mineral; active ones become glassy, wetly reflective, and warm as living flesh. Fine branching veins move beneath the dark surface. A mine is useful because it conceals the process, controls access, and holds prisoners—not because any mining machinery powers the conversion.

## Victim States

### Unprocessed

Restrained, exhausted, and frightened, but recoverable through ordinary rescue.

### Newly Impaled

Still aware and not fully converted. This is the narrow heroic intervention case. Removing the spike causes catastrophic trauma; survival requires immediate critical medical or magical healing **and** a meaningful way to break or purge the curse in the blood.

### Progressing

Dark branching marks spread, speech fails, pain response changes, and voluntary control becomes intermittent. Removal remains physically possible but survival and restoration should demand exceptional resources and judgment.

### Fully Converted

A silent thrall under Legion command. Restoration, if possible at all, belongs beyond the scope of the one-shot and should require major magic or a later cure.

## Removal Is Not Rescue

Never present spike extraction as a single successful check that returns a victim to normal. The spike has created an impalement wound large enough to kill, and cursed blood continues the conversion after removal. A successful rescue combines:

1. controlled extraction or severing;
2. immediate stabilization of catastrophic bleeding and organ damage;
3. powerful healing;
4. curse-breaking, cleansing, or equivalent magic;
5. continued care after escape.

Reward resource-intensive, imaginative intervention. The doctrine exists to preserve the horror and urgency, not to forbid the players from achieving something extraordinary.
